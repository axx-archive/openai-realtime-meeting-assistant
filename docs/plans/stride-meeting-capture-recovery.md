# Meeting capture and recovery: first implementation slice

Status: decision-complete implementation proposal, 2026-09-04. No media implementation, provider call, production change, or physical-device qualification was performed for this design. The inherited transcription path is a prototype and regression base. This slice qualifies recoverable capture; it does not qualify meeting comprehension or agent participation.

## Decision and outcome

Build one durable, consent-bound stream per admitted microphone publication, immediately after the existing server decoder and before the mixer's bounded intake and speech gate. Keep the SFU RTP forwarding owner, decoder, admission authority, and one transcription dispatcher per source. Replace the new stream's direct source-to-provider path with journal-driven dispatch; never run both persistence paths for the same source. Mixed Scout audio remains separately consent-gated and cannot become an authoritative transcript fallback.

The first demonstration has two endpoints: source A keeps transcribing while source B loses its provider connection for 30 seconds. B's authorized, durably accepted audio survives, is replayed with stable segment identity, and produces one final transcript per segment. B remains visibly behind until its own backlog clears. Every unrecoverable interval is retained as source-specific coverage evidence, including short gaps. A healthy peer cannot erase B's degradation.

The accounting boundary starts at server receipt, not the physical microphone. RTP never received, decoder loss, quiet classification, audio deliberately excluded by consent, and durable audio awaiting transcription are different conditions. Do not call an interval complete merely because every known final transcript has a capture sequence.

## Ownership and contracts

**Source identity.** Allocate a server-generated `captureStreamId` after exact publication admission. Bind it to canonical tenant/organization mapping, room, sitting, admission anchor, participant principal, publication incarnation, original media generation, recording epoch and channel role `microphone`. Track ID/SSRC and display name alone are not identities. A track replacement, new seat, recording epoch or changed durable consent record set starts a new stream. A provider reconnect does not. Participant identity names the endpoint's admitted principal; shared microphones do not thereby obtain diarization.

**Time and provenance.** Extend RTP sequence/timestamps per publication, recording wrap/reorder events. Store decoded sample offsets at 48 kHz mono, observed wall-clock bounds, clock mapping revision, codec and decoder revision. Every offered decoded frame obtains its immutable sample range before nonblocking queue admission. Track decoder failure, RTP discontinuity, and rejected queue ranges separately. Never turn packet counts into exact missing-word counts; uncertain RTP duration is an uncertainty interval. Transport silence, mute and source-ended signals are distinct from a failed provider.

**Transient payload.** Use one-second PCM16 chunks (96,000 bytes maximum at 48 kHz mono), flushing a partial chunk at source/epoch end. Preserve audio before the speech gate. Dispatch segmentation may use a deterministic, versioned gate with 300 ms pre-roll; do not overlap segment-owned sample ranges. Quiet ranges become `excluded_gate` only after the pre-roll window closes and are reported as algorithmically excluded, not proof of no speech. Initial local fixtures should also run with gating disabled to distinguish capture defects from selection defects.

**Durable state.** Reuse the deployed PostgreSQL infrastructure for body-free stream/chunk/segment/attempt metadata and conditional updates. Put encrypted chunk files in a dedicated service-only transient volume mounted at `/app/capture-spool`, outside `meeting-memory.jsonl`, public uploads and normal backup scope. Do not add another database or new external service for this slice. Proposed tables:

- `meeting_capture_streams`: immutable binding, original consent record IDs/digest/policy and cutoff, audience/ACL/purge revision, recording epoch, format, lifecycle, reservation high-water, durable sample high-water, last capture heartbeat, retention deadline.
- `meeting_capture_chunks`: unique `(stream_id, chunk_sequence)`, exact sample interval, ciphertext reference/digest, disposition, reason and timestamps. Unique source intervals reject payload changes under the same ID.
- `meeting_capture_segments`: unique stable segment ID, ordered chunk subranges, segmentation revision, input digest, state, current attempt generation, final transcript ID/revision/digest or explicit no-text receipt. Attempts retain provider-generation/request binding and bounded retry counters; they never carry authority inferred from a provider item ID.
- `meeting_capture_events`: body-free terminal gaps, source closure, withdrawal/purge and receipt-reconciliation records. Consent denial does not store speaker audio or sensitive content merely to explain exclusion.

The spool worker revalidates both audio-capture and transcription authority under the existing consent commit fence before persisting eligible payload; queued local consent is not sufficient. Write ciphertext to a temporary file, fsync it, rename and fsync its directory, then commit the chunk reference and durable acknowledgement. A concurrent withdrawal is ordered against this write, and denial removes any temporary ciphertext without publishing acceptance. Database failure leaves an unreferenced encrypted orphan for bounded cleanup, never an accepted chunk. Missing or corrupt referenced ciphertext becomes an explicit unrecoverable range. A chunk is only `durable` after both operations complete. The RTP goroutine never waits on disk, SQL, network or a model.

Use a dedicated encryption key, never a consent MAC/signing key. Use authenticated encryption with unique per-chunk nonces; bind tenant, source, sample range, format and original consent digest as associated data. A per-stream data key is wrapped under that key; wrapped-key files and ciphertext share the excluded transient spool. Normal access is service-only, directory mode 0700 and file mode 0600. The first pilot must verify backup exclusion and retention cleanup; encryption does not itself prove physical secure erasure. Body-free purge tombstones remain durable outside the spool and block restored ciphertext. A restored database/spool cannot resume until current consent/purge authority is reachable and reconciled. Key loss produces gaps, never an attempt to bypass encryption.

**Nonblocking acceptance and crash honesty.** A bounded RAM queue offer is `observed`, not `durable`. Limit it to two seconds per source and record rejected exact sample intervals through an independent coalescing metadata channel. Persist source-open and reserve sequence/time blocks before admitting durable capture; keep an unclean-close marker and one-second durable heartbeat. If the process dies before rejection metadata or the current partial chunk is durable, report the interval from the last durable watermark through the last reserved/observed boundary as `unknown_after_crash`. Do not claim all observed frames survived. An unclosed stream with no trustworthy end boundary remains explicitly open-ended until authoritative source-end/rejoin evidence bounds it. Full source-to-device capture guarantees require a separate client acknowledgement protocol and are outside this slice.

## Dispatch, publication and recovery

1. One leased dispatcher owns a stream's transcription, with a monotonically fenced attempt generation. PostgreSQL compare-and-swap prevents two workers from publishing the same segment. One active provider session per source remains the runtime rule. No mixed fallback acquires transcript persistence ownership.
2. Persist a segment plan and exact original input range before sending any audio. Commit at the existing 800 ms silence/15-second ceiling initially, but derive occurred times from sample ranges, not reconnect time. Provider append/commit writes are delivery attempts, never durable transcript acknowledgements.
3. Reauthorize original capture records immediately before payload decryption/dispatch. Current allowance must cover the original recording/admission boundary, and original records must not have been withdrawn/superseded. A later grant does not authorize earlier denied or withdrawn audio. Reconnect increments provider generation only. Reconstruct provider item bindings from the exact segment attempt, never FIFO completion order across connections.
4. On lost connection after append or commit, mark the attempt outcome unknown. Reconcile an already persisted exact transcript first. If absent and still authorized, retry the same stable segment with a new attempt generation, within the replay limits. Repeated external transcription is possible; exactly-once provider billing is not promised. Late old-generation completion cannot overwrite a newer final or create another transcript.
5. Publish through the existing known-source transcript store under current consent locks, source processing capability, current audience and purge fence. Use a deterministic transcript key derived from stream+segment and compare existing input/result binding before any append. A matching durable result returns its receipt; a conflicting result fails closed. Only after the transcript write is durable may journal acknowledgement proceed. A chunk shared by adjacent segment subranges is deleted only when every owned subrange has a terminal disposition; no early deletion through the first completed segment is allowed. Crash after transcript append but before acknowledgement is repaired by exact receipt lookup, without another provider call. Crash before append may retry; transient provider text is not represented as a final result.
6. A successful empty provider final is `no_text`, not `transcribed`, and retains input accounting. It must not hide a long speech-classified interval; that becomes a quality warning. A provider error remains pending/retryable or ends as an explicit gap. Repeated words across genuine adjacent segments remain an accuracy issue; stable IDs prevent duplicate segment publication, not every possible lexical repetition.

### Restart and source closure authority

A saved process-local fence is never reused after boot. Introduce a narrow durable `CapturedAudioProcessingReceipt`, minted while the stream is admitted: exact source binding, original consent IDs/policy, allowed sample ceiling, retention deadline, audience and purge revision. It authorizes processing only already-captured eligible samples; it cannot receive new RTP, join a room, impersonate a participant, or route a result into another sitting. Its current validator re-resolves consent/cutoff and purge state and checks the receipt's immutable capture boundary.

Ordinary source disconnect, process restart, or stopping recording seals capture; it can drain previously accepted authorized samples until their original TTL. Withdrawal, deletion, admission invalidation for security, and explicit stop-and-discard revoke processing. Starting recording again never extends old stream deadlines. A restart that cannot distinguish orderly end from revoked admission fails closed until authority reconciliation; expiry then records a gap. If the legacy publisher requires the *current* live media generation, add a narrow historical transcript commit mode validated by this receipt instead of weakening the live generation check. Historical completion targets only its original sitting and is not broadcast as new speech in a successor room.

This processing receipt and its validator are required in the first slice. Without them, durable audio across restart is only stored data, not safely replayable work.

## Bounds and observable state

Initial pilot limits, explicit engineering choices to validate: 120 seconds of unacknowledged audio per stream, 16 concurrent source streams per room, 256 MiB per room and 1 GiB per process spool ceiling, two in-flight segments per source, three attempts per segment including the first. Retry delays start at 1 second and cap at 8 seconds within the original 120-second deadline. Replay is fair across sources and bounded to 2× audio duration per wall-clock second, subject to provider admission. Current audio is journaled while older segments drain; no source's backlog may block another's writes. Reaching any limit creates a reasoned range gap and releases the payload; it never silently evicts audio or increases the allowance. These are capture/storage bounds, not business-value metrics.

Source states: `not_recording`, `excluded_consent`, `waiting_for_audio`, `capturing`, `catching_up`, `degraded`, `ended`. Keep coverage as separate ranges with dispositions `transcribed`, `no_text`, `excluded_gate`, `excluded_consent`, `revoked`, `expired`, `missing`, `unknown_after_crash`. Transitions are source-versioned so reordered client events cannot restore stale health. A detected delivery failure, rejected capture range, or overdue durable acknowledgement degrades that source within 5 seconds; normal 15-second provider segmentation alone does not trigger it. The room is complete only when every relevant source range is accounted for, with exclusions and gaps displayed honestly. A recovered connection is not recovered words.

No gap-duration floor. Coalesce adjacent identical reasons for presentation while retaining exact sample ranges. Export counters/durations by source internally and aggregate low-cardinality metrics externally: observed/durable/excluded/lost samples, oldest unacknowledged age, queue bytes, replay seconds/attempts, persistence latency, finalization latency, stale completion rejections, and payload-deletion lag. Do not emit transcript text, account identity or audio in logs or readiness responses.

## Corrections, withdrawal and purge

- Withdrawal is linearized with dispatch/publication using the existing binding locks and durable consent records. Stop new capture, cancel owned attempts, invalidate queued payload eligibility and prevent late results. Physical payload cleanup follows; readers deny immediately even if unlink fails. No provider deletion guarantee is inferred from closing a websocket.
- A corrected final transcript keeps its stable segment identity with a new transcript revision/digest. Journal receipt reconciliation recognizes that it has been superseded and never replays audio to restore the old wording. Dependent source episodes, answers and meeting facts must lose “current” eligibility before the corrected source is exposed as current; rebuild only from eligible exact revisions.
- Purge advances a durable monotonic stream/source generation first. All readers/dispatchers/publication paths check it. Delete ciphertext and wrapped keys, invalidate receipts and derived references, and retain only non-content minimal tombstones allowed by retention. A failed cleanup is reported and retried without making data readable. Re-importing an older spool or metadata backup cannot lower the current purge generation.
- Raw audio expires at its original deadline or immediately after durable final acknowledgement. Corrections after expiry operate on transcript provenance and human evidence; this slice does not create indefinite meeting recordings.

## Implementation ownership and rollout

New files: `meeting_capture_journal.go` (contracts), `meeting_capture_postgres.go` plus one additive migration (metadata/CAS), `meeting_capture_spool.go` (encrypted files/cleanup), `meeting_capture_authority.go` (durable capture-processing receipt/current validator), `meeting_capture_replay.go` (single dispatcher and receipt reconciliation), and focused corresponding tests. Exact migration number is allocated at implementation time to avoid colliding with current business migrations.

Bounded existing edits: `main.go` tap and decoder admission separation; `audio_mixer.go` removal of the gated transcript path only for journal-owned streams; `transcription_lane.go` provider adapter/segment bindings; `room_live.go` per-source projection; existing known-source transcript append/correction and source-episode coverage hooks. Web/native consume the same versioned source coverage payload. No camera/microphone owner change, second decoder, STT model change, Scout voice enablement or analysis-model redesign is included.

Land the journal, authority validator, fake provider and crash tests first. Then integrate one explicitly enabled local sitting. Ownership mode is fixed at source-open and persisted; flag changes apply only to new streams. No automatic fallback from journal failure to the old transcript owner. Rollback stops dispatch, retains denial/gap evidence and expires pending payload; an old binary must not consume the new spool. The feature remains disabled for production until storage/key/consent configuration, source compatibility, rendered coverage and physical media-isolation gates pass. The existing markdown→digest pipeline can consume new transcript revisions only with explicit coverage; its semantic quality remains a separate qualification.

The product design owner supplies the canonical typography, spacing, surfaces, icon meanings and interaction language for web and iOS. This capture slice contributes state contracts, not an independent visual system. The same names, meanings and recovery actions must appear on both; native permission sheets and platform screen-broadcast pickers retain their platform behavior.

Join produces one admitted publication per enabled device and truthful local/remote state; tapping twice or returning from a permission sheet cannot create a duplicate owner. Loading and failed admission do not appear connected. |
| Device and privacy controls | Mute, camera and recording/transcription controls remain reachable with captions and keyboard open. Rendered acceptance requires screenshots and actual interactions through these states on each platform; visual polish is not evidence of acoustic quality. Physical acceptance separately measures join success/time-to-first-audio/video, conversational latency, audio interruption and echo, video freeze duration, reconnect recovery time, screen-share start/stop and audio/video synchronization. Capture-journal metrics cannot substitute for these call metrics. Record baseline and candidate distributions under the same network/device scenarios; no Zoom-parity claim follows from this slice alone.
